Interviewer: Thanks for making time, Aunty Bola. I know the lunch rush just ended. Can you tell me a bit about the restaurant?
Bola: No problem at all, my dear. It is called Mama Bola's Kitchen. I started it nine years ago with two pots and one gas cooker. Now I have six staff, and we serve about sixty plates on a good day. Amala, ewedu, rice and stew, pepper soup on weekends. Mostly office people from the area and some students.
Interviewer: That's impressive. So, I'm working on a mobile app for restaurant owners. Before I say anything about it, can you walk me through how you keep track of your stock today? Things like rice, tomatoes, palm oil, meat.
Bola: Ah, okay. Every night when we close, I write down what came in and what is left in a big notebook. My younger sister Kemi sits with me and we check it against the receipts, and it takes about an hour. Then in the morning the cook tells me what is finished and I buy again at the market.
Interviewer: And does that notebook always match what is on the shelf?
Bola: Not always. Last month we ran out of rice on a Friday afternoon, and Friday is my best day. The notebook said we had two bags. When we checked, one bag had been used for a naming ceremony order that nobody wrote down. So I had to send the boy to buy rice at the retail price, and some customers had already left.
Interviewer: How much did that cost you, roughly?
Bola: I did not calculate it. Maybe the plates we lost, and the extra money for the rice. It is not a thing I can say exactly.
Interviewer: Okay. Wouldn't it be great if an app counted your stock for you, so you never ran out on a Friday again?
Bola: Oh, that would be fantastic. I would definitely pay for something like that. That is a brilliant idea, my dear.
Interviewer: How much would you pay, do you think?
Bola: Haha, you are asking me price now. Let me see. If it is not too costly, I can pay two or three thousand naira a month. But I would have to see it first. These things sometimes look nice on the phone and then nobody uses them.
Interviewer: Have you used any app for the business before?
Bola: Only the bank app, and WhatsApp. I get my orders on WhatsApp. We have never used a special app for the kitchen or the stock. My nephew tried to set up something for me once, but I did not have time for it.
Interviewer: What happened with that?
Bola: Nothing. He put it on my phone, I opened it two times and I forgot. The shop was too busy that month.
Interviewer: Who would use the app in your place? You, or the staff?
Bola: Me, mostly. The cook, Mama Ngozi, does not like phones. She is sixty-two. The staff can learn if you teach them slowly. But I would be the one entering things.
Interviewer: And would you enter the deliveries every time they come in?
Bola: Yes, I would enter everything, every single day. Because it would save me the hour I spend with Kemi at night. Actually, I want that hour back, because Kemi has her own work.
Interviewer: When you say you want that hour back, what would you do with it?
Bola: Rest, my dear. Or prepare for the next day. I wake up at four in the morning.
Interviewer: Let me ask about money. How do you pay your suppliers?
Bola: Mostly cash, some transfer. The rice woman and the meat seller, I pay them on Saturdays for the whole week. The others I pay on the spot.
Interviewer: What do you spend on your phone every month, data and so on?
Bola: About three thousand naira on data. I use it for orders and for my daughter in Canada. So if the app is another three thousand, hmm, that is double. I must be sure.
Interviewer: That's helpful to know. Do other owners you know have the same problem with stock?
Bola: I think most buka owners are suffering the same way, because everybody uses notebook. Some of them are even worse than me. They keep it in their head.
Interviewer: So let me understand, you would use this and you would pay for it?
Bola: Yes, I will use it, and I will tell my friends in the market association about it. You are doing a good thing, young man.
Interviewer: Is there anything that would stop you from using it?
Bola: Network, maybe. Sometimes the network disappears in the afternoon. And light. If there is no light I cannot charge my phone. But that is Lagos, we manage.
Interviewer: Last question. If I came back in two months with a working version, would you try it?
Bola: Of course. Bring it, I will try it, you can even come and sit in the shop. And bring the price list.
Interviewer: Thank you so much, Aunty Bola. I really appreciate your time.
Bola: You are welcome. God will bless your work. Go and eat something before you go, there is rice in the pot.
