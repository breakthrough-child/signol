Interviewer: Thanks for meeting me, Chinedu. Can you tell me about your canteen?
Chinedu: Sure, no wahala. It is a small place near the university gate. Twelve seats inside, and a lot of takeaway. I do jollof, fried rice, beans and plantain, some chicken. Students mostly. I opened three years ago after I left my banking job. My uncle lent me the money for the first set of equipment.
Interviewer: And who works with you?
Chinedu: Two cooks and one girl who serves. I am there from six in the morning till we finish, about nine at night.
Interviewer: I'm building a mobile app that helps restaurant owners manage their inventory. How do you handle your stock today?
Chinedu: Honestly, I carry most of it in my head. Every morning before the cooks start I walk through the store and look at what is low. Then I write a shopping list on my phone and go to Yaba market or Mile 12, depending on what I need.
Interviewer: Don't you hate it when you run out of an ingredient in the middle of service?
Chinedu: Ha, who doesn't? Of course, it is the worst feeling. But to be honest, it does not happen to me often. Maybe once in a month, and then we just tell students that the beans is finished.
Interviewer: So it's a problem but you have a way around it.
Chinedu: When something finishes, we sell the other things and the students still buy. It is not a big thing for me. The small things that finish are normally the extras, like plantain.
Interviewer: Right. If an app tracked everything automatically so you never ran out, how would that feel?
Chinedu: It sounds like a very good idea. Technology is the future, my friend, I always say so. I used to work in a bank, so I like systems.
Interviewer: Would you use it?
Chinedu: I would probably try it if it is cheap. Maybe a free month first, then I can see.
Interviewer: What would "cheap" mean to you?
Chinedu: Not more than one thousand, one thousand five hundred naira a month. Every kobo counts for a small place like this one.
Interviewer: Okay. Have you ever paid for any software or app for the business?
Chinedu: No, never. Everything I use is free. WhatsApp, the phone notes, and Google Sheets for my accounts.
Interviewer: Tell me about the Google Sheets. How do you use it?
Chinedu: On Sunday I enter the week's sales and the expenses, and I check if I made profit. It takes me about thirty minutes. I learned it at the bank.
Interviewer: And do you put the stock in there as well?
Chinedu: No. Stock is just in my head. For a place this size, I don't think it is necessary.
Interviewer: Hmm. What if you had a second branch? Would it change things?
Chinedu: Ah, then yes. If I open a second place, I will need something like that. But that one is far, maybe next year, maybe the year after.
Interviewer: Do you know other owners around here who struggle with stock?
Chinedu: I think many of them do, especially the ones who are not around all day. But I cannot say for sure. I don't talk about money with the other owners.
Interviewer: Tell me about the last time a supplier changed your prices. What did you do?
Chinedu: Two months ago the tomato seller in Mile 12 increased the price by almost half, so I called my cousin who sells in Oyingbo and I bought from him for two weeks. I did not need any app for that. It was a phone call. After the two weeks the Mile 12 price came back down and I went back to her, because she gives me credit.
Interviewer: So credit matters a lot to you.
Chinedu: Very much. I take about one third of my ingredients on credit and I pay on Saturday after the weekend sales. If anything on my phone showed me all that, it would be more useful than the stock thing, to be honest.
Interviewer: Interesting. Say more about that.
Chinedu: I mean, who I owe, how much, when. Sometimes I forget small small amounts and then the seller reminds me in front of other customers. That is embarrassing, and it has happened to me at least three times this year. If you build something for that, I would take it more seriously.
Interviewer: And how do you decide how much to order each week?
Chinedu: By feeling. Monday and Tuesday are slow, Thursday and Friday the students come out in full, so I order more for those days. After three years, I know my business.
Interviewer: Do your cooks ever complain about shortages?
Chinedu: Not really. They know to tell me early. The big cook, Emeka, always says, boss, the oil is almost finished, and I buy it the same day.
Interviewer: If I send you a link when the app is ready, will you try it?
Chinedu: Yes, send it to me, I will have a look. You are a serious guy, I like what you are doing.
Interviewer: Is there anything that would make it hard for you to use?
Chinedu: Data. I keep my data off most of the day to save money. I only turn it on when I need to send a message or check my bank. So an app that needs internet all the time would be a problem.
Interviewer: That's good to know. Anything else I should ask?
Chinedu: Maybe ask the people who sell in bigger quantity. For me, it is small small. But good luck, I believe in you.
Interviewer: Thanks, Chinedu. Have a good rest of the day.
Chinedu: You too. Come and eat jollof before you go, it is on the house.
