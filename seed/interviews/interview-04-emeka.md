Interviewer: Emeka, thank you for the time. I know you are on your feet all day. What's the setup here?
Emeka: No problem. This place is called Emeka's Table, but everyone calls it Mama Put, because my mother started it. We are in Lekki Phase One, behind the plaza. Twenty-eight seats, and we do a lot of delivery orders on the food apps. I took it over from my mother four years ago when she got sick. She is better now but she only comes to cook the soups on Saturdays.
Interviewer: How many people work with you?
Emeka: Nine. Three cooks, two kitchen assistants, three servers, and one rider for our own deliveries.
Interviewer: I'm working on an app for restaurant inventory. Surely tracking stock by hand is a nightmare for you?
Emeka: Ah, you have said it very well. Everybody in this business knows that stock is a headache.
Interviewer: How do you track yours right now?
Emeka: Last year I built a spreadsheet for the store, with all the items and the prices, and I used it for three weeks and then I stopped. I think it was too much typing. After service I am tired, and the cooks do not want to wait.
Interviewer: What happened to the stock after you stopped?
Emeka: We went back to the old way, which is looking at the shelf and asking the kitchen assistants. My kitchen assistant, Samuel, tells me every evening what is low, and I order the next day.
Interviewer: How often do things run out?
Emeka: Not too often. Maybe once or twice a month something finishes, like crayfish or egusi, and I send the rider to the market. It is annoying but it is not a disaster.
Interviewer: So what would change if you had the app?
Emeka: I love the idea. It is very modern, and I like modern things. I will definitely download it when it is ready.
Interviewer: And would you pay for it?
Emeka: I would pay if it is something that really helps, yes. But you know, I already pay for so many things. The food apps take twenty-five percent of every order, and the POS has its own monthly fee.
Interviewer: Tell me about the food apps. How do you manage orders from them?
Emeka: They send the order to a tablet, and my server confirms it, and then the kitchen prepares it. Sometimes a customer orders something we already finished, and we have to cancel, and they give us a bad rating. That is actually the thing that hurts me more than the stock.
Interviewer: Say more about that.
Emeka: When an item is finished, I have to go to the tablet and switch it off. If I forget, we get three or four cancelled orders and the app reduces my ranking. It happened twice in December.
Interviewer: So if the app knew what was in stock, it could switch items off for you?
Emeka: That would be very nice. If it can connect to the food app, I would pay good money for it. But I do not think those apps allow other apps to connect to them.
Interviewer: Okay. Do other owners around Lekki have the same problems?
Emeka: I believe most restaurants in Lekki don't track their stock at all. They are just guessing. Some of them are doing well anyway.
Interviewer: And what about you? Do you think you need better tracking?
Emeka: I think I could do better, but I am not sure that I need an app for it. If I had more time, maybe I would sit down and fix the spreadsheet.
Interviewer: What did the spreadsheet look like? What did you put in it?
Emeka: I had one column for the item, one for the quantity, one for the price, and one for the date I bought it. I even made it colourful. The problem was that every delivery needed me to open the laptop, and the laptop stays at home.
Interviewer: So the laptop was the obstacle.
Emeka: Partly. The bigger thing is that the numbers were never right the next day. Samuel would use something from the store at night and not tell me, so my sheet said we had more than we really had. After two or three days I stopped believing the sheet, and then I stopped opening it.
Interviewer: How did you feel when you stopped?
Emeka: Relieved, if I am honest. When you track stock, it feels like you are always behind. Now I just walk in and look.
Interviewer: Let me ask about the delivery apps again. How much of your revenue comes through them?
Emeka: About a third of our sales come from the food apps now, and it keeps growing. That is why the cancelled orders worry me. Every cancelled order is money and ranking.
Interviewer: Would you check an app on your phone every evening, as part of closing the shop?
Emeka: Yes, if it is simple, I can do it before I close. But I told you, I said the same thing about the spreadsheet in the beginning.
Interviewer: Would you share your old spreadsheet with me? It would help me understand how you thought about it.
Emeka: Sure, I will send it. You are very thorough, I like that.
Interviewer: One last thing. If you had to pick, what is the most annoying part of running the kitchen?
Emeka: Honestly? Staff turnover. Every three months somebody leaves and I have to train another person from zero. So any app must be simple enough that a new person can learn it in one day.

Emeka: My pleasure. Take some chin chin before you go.
