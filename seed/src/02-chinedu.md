# Interview 2: Chinedu, canteen owner in Yaba

Interviewer: Thanks for meeting me, Chinedu. Can you tell me about your canteen?
Chinedu: Sure, no wahala. It is a small place near the university gate. Twelve seats inside, and a lot of takeaway. I do jollof, fried rice, beans and plantain, some chicken. Students mostly. I opened three years ago after I left my banking job. My uncle lent me the money for the first set of equipment.
Interviewer: And who works with you?
Chinedu: Two cooks and one girl who serves. I am there from six in the morning till we finish, about nine at night.
Interviewer: I'm building a mobile app that helps restaurant owners manage their inventory. How do you handle your stock today?
Chinedu: Honestly, I carry most of it in my head. {{pb0|A concrete description of his current method: memory plus a quick look at the shelves.|Every morning before the cooks start I walk through the store and look at what is low.}} Then I write a shopping list on my phone and go to Yaba market or Mile 12, depending on what I need.
Interviewer: {{lq|It presupposes that running out is hated and invites a polite yes.|When was the last time an ingredient ran out in the middle of service? What did you do?|Don't you hate it when you run out of an ingredient in the middle of service?}}
Chinedu: Ha, who doesn't? {{cm0|Polite agreement with a leading question; says nothing specific.|Of course, it is the worst feeling.}} But to be honest, it does not happen to me often. Maybe once in a month, and then we just tell students that the beans is finished.
Interviewer: So it's a problem but you have a way around it.
Chinedu: {{cp0|A real but minor, infrequent issue the owner has already absorbed.|When something finishes, we sell the other things and the students still buy.}} It is not a big thing for me. The small things that finish are normally the extras, like plantain.
Interviewer: Right. If an app tracked everything automatically so you never ran out, how would that feel?
Chinedu: {{cm0|Generic praise for the concept.|It sounds like a very good idea.}} {{cm0|Praise with no commitment.|Technology is the future, my friend, I always say so.}} I used to work in a bank, so I like systems.
Interviewer: Would you use it?
Chinedu: {{fp+|A soft, conditional promise; no price or timing.|I would probably try it if it is cheap.}} Maybe a free month first, then I can see.
Interviewer: What would "cheap" mean to you?
Chinedu: Not more than one thousand, one thousand five hundred naira a month. {{og0|A general remark about what students and owners accept, not about him.|Every kobo counts for a small place like this one.}}
Interviewer: Okay. Have you ever paid for any software or app for the business?
Chinedu: {{pb-|A concrete past behavior: he has never paid for a business app, which is relevant to willingness to pay monthly.|No, never. Everything I use is free. WhatsApp, the phone notes, and Google Sheets for my accounts.}}
Interviewer: Tell me about the Google Sheets. How do you use it?
Chinedu: {{pb0|Describes real, current record-keeping for sales (not stock).|On Sunday I enter the week's sales and the expenses, and I check if I made profit.}} It takes me about thirty minutes. I learned it at the bank.
Interviewer: And do you put the stock in there as well?
Chinedu: No. Stock is just in my head. {{og0|Opinion that stock tracking is not worth the effort for him.|For a place this size, I don't think it is necessary.}}
Interviewer: Hmm. What if you had a second branch? Would it change things?
Chinedu: Ah, then yes. {{fp+|A conditional promise tied to a hypothetical second branch.|If I open a second place, I will need something like that.}} But that one is far, maybe next year, maybe the year after.
Interviewer: Do you know other owners around here who struggle with stock?
Chinedu: {{og+|A claim about other people with no specific example.|I think many of them do, especially the ones who are not around all day.}} But I cannot say for sure. I don't talk about money with the other owners.
Interviewer: Tell me about the last time a supplier changed your prices. What did you do?
Chinedu: {{pb0|Describes how he actually reacts to cost changes: calls around and switches suppliers.|Two months ago the tomato seller in Mile 12 increased the price by almost half, so I called my cousin who sells in Oyingbo and I bought from him for two weeks.}} I did not need any app for that. It was a phone call. After the two weeks the Mile 12 price came back down and I went back to her, because she gives me credit.
Interviewer: So credit matters a lot to you.
Chinedu: Very much. {{pb0|A concrete financing habit: buys on credit and pays on weekends.|I take about one third of my ingredients on credit and I pay on Saturday after the weekend sales.}} If anything on my phone showed me all that, it would be more useful than the stock thing, to be honest.
Interviewer: Interesting. Say more about that.
Chinedu: I mean, who I owe, how much, when. Sometimes I forget small small amounts and then the seller reminds me in front of other customers. {{cp0|A specific, recurring pain, but about debts to suppliers rather than stock levels.|That is embarrassing, and it has happened to me at least three times this year.}} If you build something for that, I would take it more seriously.
Interviewer: And how do you decide how much to order each week?
Chinedu: By feeling. {{og0|His own rule of thumb, stated as opinion.|Monday and Tuesday are slow, Thursday and Friday the students come out in full, so I order more for those days.}} After three years, I know my business.
Interviewer: Do your cooks ever complain about shortages?
Chinedu: Not really. They know to tell me early. {{cp0|Says shortages are rare and handled by the cooks speaking up.|The big cook, Emeka, always says, boss, the oil is almost finished, and I buy it the same day.}}
Interviewer: If I send you a link when the app is ready, will you try it?
Chinedu: {{fp+|Agrees to try a free link; costs him nothing.|Yes, send it to me, I will have a look.}} {{cm0|Courtesy.|You are a serious guy, I like what you are doing.}}
Interviewer: Is there anything that would make it hard for you to use?
Chinedu: Data. I keep my data off most of the day to save money. {{cp0|A concrete constraint on app usage that he describes as part of daily routine.|I only turn it on when I need to send a message or check my bank.}} So an app that needs internet all the time would be a problem.
Interviewer: That's good to know. Anything else I should ask?
Chinedu: Maybe ask the people who sell in bigger quantity. For me, it is small small. {{cm0|Encouraging but empty remark.|But good luck, I believe in you.}}
Interviewer: Thanks, Chinedu. Have a good rest of the day.
Chinedu: You too. Come and eat jollof before you go, it is on the house.
